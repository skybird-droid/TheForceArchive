Three Years on the Front Lines: How The Force Was Born

Posted on October 16, 1996 by Skybird

Three years. It feels like a lifetime ago, yet I can still hear the roar of the crowd in downtown Toronto like it was yesterday.

On October 16, 1993, seven strange individuals stepped out of the shadows and onto a stage, changing the landscape of Canadian heroics forever. Before that day, Ontario was dealing with ordinary crime and extraordinary threats with very little coordination. We had powerful people scattered across the province, but no central anchor. That all changed when Arthur Pendelton, CEO of Pendelton Global Technologies, stepped up to give us something we desperately needed: unity.

Pendelton spent millions building the infrastructure, the comms, and the logistics to keep the team operational. I remember standing in the press crowd, listening to his booming voice over the loudspeakers.

Arthur Pendelton’s Opening Remarks (Oct 16, 1993)

Ladies and gentlemen, distinguished guests, and citizens of Canada. Today, we close the chapter on uncertain times. Our nation’s growth has brought unprecedented challenges, and traditional security is no longer enough to safeguard our communities. It takes a unique strength to meet these modern threats—a force of nature, driven by purpose and dedicated to protection. Today, Pendelton PGT is proud to sponsor and introduce Ontario’s first dedicated heroic initiative. I give you... The Force!

When the applause settled, the crowd went silent as the newly named leader of the team stepped up to the podium. Now, most people expected a towering, intimidating commander. Instead, out stepped The Siren.

The moment he opened his mouth, his distinct New Brunswick accent cut right through the crisp autumn air—smooth, lyrical, and carrying that unmistakable Maritime cadence. You could hear the gentle, rolling lilt in every sentence, but despite the down-home charm of his voice, there was an unmistakable undercurrent of raw authority. With his silver tongue and tactical sharpness, he commanded the entire crowd without raising his voice once.

The Siren’s Inaugural Speech (Oct 16, 1993)

Thank you, Mr. Pendelton, and thank you, Toronto. You know, coming from back home out East, we always say that a house is only as strong as the storm it can weather together. Ontario isn't just a province; it’s the heartbeat of this country. And when the heartbeat is threatened, you don't just stand by and watch. The six people standing behind me—Flex Magnus, Volt Dude, Fracture, Grievance, Jump-Cut, and Sir Runz-A-Lot—are as different as night and day. We have our quirks, and Lord knows we aren't perfect. But when duty calls, we stand as one wall against whatever comes our way. We’re here for Ontario, and we’re here for Canada. You’re safe on our watch.

Looking back over these past three years, they’ve more than lived up to that promise.

They started right here in Ontario, keeping the streets safe and shutting down rogue threats from Windsor to Ottawa. But their reach hasn't stopped at the provincial border. When crisis struck the Prairies, Sir Runz-A-Lot was across the border before the local authorities even finished making the emergency call. When heavy infrastructure collapsed during the ice storms out East, Grievance was there anchoring entire bridge supports with his telekinesis while Fracture pulled civilians out of the debris, taking hits that would destroy anyone else and shaking them off minutes later.

We’ve seen Volt Dude power critical hospital backups during blackouts, Flex Magnus stretch across chasm gaps to evacuate stranded commuters, and Jump-Cut quietly neutralize high-risk hostage situations before the media even knew what was happening.

They aren't an easy group to manage—between Flex's massive ego, Jump-Cut accidentally teleporting into the wrong rooms, and Sir Runz-A-Lot running headfirst into walls because his eyes can't keep up with his legs—they are as chaotic as they are brilliant. But under The Siren's watchful eye, it works.

Happy 3rd Anniversary to The Force. Here’s to many more years of keeping our home safe.

— Skybird
